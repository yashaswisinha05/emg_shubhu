% Methodology for the current minimal two-stage TIDE model.
% Required packages: amsmath, amssymb, bm

\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

We consider causal estimation of gripper state, current hand position, screen
location, and short-horizon motion from four surface electromyography (sEMG)
channels and four six-axis inertial measurement units (IMUs). At time $t$, the
raw wearable observation is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_t=[\mathbf{e}_t,\mathbf{a}_t,\boldsymbol{\omega}_t],
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{4}$ contains the sEMG measurements and
$\mathbf{a}_t,\boldsymbol{\omega}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{12}$ contain acceleration and
angular velocity from the four IMUs. Using only observations available at or
before $t$, the deployed model predicts the open/close state $\hat g_t$, current
Cartesian position $\hat{\mathbf p}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$, normalized screen
location $\hat{\mathbf u}_t\in[0,1]^2$, and future Cartesian positions
$\hat{\mathbf p}_{t+h\mid t}$ at $h\in\{10,20,\ldots,200\}$ ms. Orientation,
final endpoint, spatial-cell, within-cell offset, and one-second Cartesian
prediction heads are not part of the final model.

The method uses two training stages but does not deploy two duplicate feature
extractors. Stage I learns modality-specific causal encoders and a robust
gripper-state classifier. Stage II freezes and evaluates these encoders once,
then reuses their features through lightweight recurrent adapters for motion
prediction. This separation preserves the validated state representation while
preventing regression losses from changing the gripper decision boundary.

\subsection{Wearable Signals and Causal Preprocessing}

The four wearable units are distributed around the forearm. Each unit provides
one sEMG channel, three-axis acceleration, and three-axis angular velocity,
giving four sEMG and 24 IMU channels. Measurements are ordered by acquisition
timestamp and resampled to a uniform 100-Hz grid. A missing measurement is
forward-filled only when the most recent past observation is no more than
20 ms old. Longer gaps remain invalid and are represented explicitly by binary
validity indicators.

Each sEMG channel is processed with a fourth-order causal Butterworth band-pass
filter with lower cutoff 20 Hz and upper cutoff
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{high}}=\min(450,0.4f_s)\ \mathrm{Hz},
\end{equation}
where $f_s$ is the acquisition rate. Trailing root-mean-square envelopes of
20 and 50 ms are then computed, producing eight sEMG activation features. The
short window retains rapid recruitment changes, whereas the longer window
provides a more stable activation estimate. Each IMU channel is processed by a
trailing three-sample median filter followed by a second-order causal 15-Hz
low-pass filter. The DC component is retained.

Means and standard deviations are estimated independently for every channel
using valid training samples only. Standardized values are clipped to
$[-20,20]$ and concatenated with the corresponding validity indicators. The
network inputs are therefore
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf e}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{16},\qquad
    \tilde{\mathbf i}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{48}.
\end{equation}
A fused loss is evaluated only when at least 75\% of both sensor modalities are
valid. VIVE position is used as training supervision only and is not an input
to either training stage or inference.

\subsection{Stage I: Causal Gripper-State Encoder}
\label{subsec:stage1}

\subsubsection{Modality-specific multiscale encoding}

sEMG and IMU are encoded separately because they measure different physical
processes and have different useful temporal scales. sEMG provides direct but
non-stationary evidence of muscle recruitment, whereas IMU supplies the
mechanical context required to distinguish grasp-related activity from
reaching, rotation, and stabilization. Early concatenation would force both
signals through identical temporal filters before their modality-specific
structure had been extracted.

Each input stream is projected to width $d=128$. A frame-resolution path uses
causal depthwise convolutions with kernels of five and nine samples, covering
50 and 90 ms at the model rate. In parallel, a context path forms overlapping
16-frame patches with stride four and passes them through a four-layer,
four-head Transformer. Left padding ensures that the token associated with
time $t$ contains no sample later than $t$, and a causal attention mask blocks
future tokens. The most recent completed patch representation is held between
successive patch endpoints and combined with the local representation.

Fusion is performed separately for the local and contextual paths. For
$r\in\{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{ctx}\}$, let $\mathbf h_t^{e,r}$ and
$\mathbf h_t^{i,r}$ denote the sEMG and IMU features. A detached gate input
produces modality weights
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{\alpha}_t^r=
 \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
 g_r\!\left(\operatorname{sg}
 [\mathbf h_t^{e,r},\mathbf h_t^{i,r}]\right)\right),
\end{equation}
and the fused representation is
\begin{align}
 \mathbf z_t^r=\phi_r\big[&
 \alpha_t^{e,r}\mathbf h_t^{e,r},
 \alpha_t^{i,r}\mathbf h_t^{i,r},\nonumber\\
 &\mathbf h_t^{e,r}-\mathbf h_t^{i,r},
 \mathbf h_t^{e,r}\odot\mathbf h_t^{i,r}\big].
\end{align}
The weighted features preserve modality-specific evidence, while the difference
and product expose cross-modal disagreement and agreement. Stop-gradient is
applied only at the gate input; downstream classification gradients continue to
train both modality encoders.

\subsubsection{State head and Stage-I objective}

The state decoder combines a stable contextual prediction with a
high-resolution local prediction,
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\ell}_t^g=
    \boldsymbol{\ell}_t^{\mathrm{ctx}}
    +\sigma(\beta)\boldsymbol{\ell}_t^{\mathrm{loc}},
\end{equation}
where the local term is produced by an additional five-frame causal depthwise
convolution. The blend parameter is initialized with $\beta=-2$, initially
favoring context while allowing training to sharpen state transitions. A
causal sEMG-only pathway provides an additional zero-initialized correction to
the state logits. It receives learned MASK state tokens at inference and is
trained through the same state objective; no masked-signal reconstruction loss
is used in the final Stage-I model.

Stage I is optimized only with class-balanced cross entropy:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm I}=\mathcal L_g
 =-\frac{1}{|\mathcal V_g|}
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal V_g}w_{g_t}
 \log\operatorname{softmax}(\boldsymbol{\ell}_t^g)_{g_t},
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal V_g$ contains valid labelled frames and
$w_c=N/(2N_c)$ compensates for class frequency. Removing pose, orientation,
endpoint, pixel, future-motion, reconstruction, and stability losses makes the
purpose of this stage unambiguous. The checkpoint with the highest validation
macro-F1 is retained, after which its classifier and both patch encoders are
frozen.

\subsection{Stage II: State-Conditioned Motion Decoder}
\label{subsec:stage2}

\subsubsection{Shared features and residual adaptation}

If the two stages use different training populations, Stage-II standardized
inputs are mapped to the frozen classifier's feature scale. For modality
$m\in\{e,i\}$,
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf z_t^{c,m}=\mathbf z_t^{m}\odot
 \frac{\boldsymbol\sigma_m}{\boldsymbol\sigma_c}
 +\frac{\boldsymbol\mu_m-\boldsymbol\mu_c}
 {\boldsymbol\sigma_c},
\end{equation}
where $(\boldsymbol\mu_m,\boldsymbol\sigma_m)$ and
$(\boldsymbol\mu_c,\boldsymbol\sigma_c)$ are the Stage-II and frozen
classifier statistics, respectively.

The frozen network is evaluated once to obtain modality-specific contextual
features $\mathbf u_t^e,\mathbf u_t^i\in\mathbb{R}^{128}$ and state
probabilities $\mathbf q_t$. Separate two-layer causal GRUs learn residual
adaptations:
\begin{align}
 \mathbf h_{1:t}^{e}&=\mathbf u_{1:t}^{e}
 +W_a^e\operatorname{GRU}_e(\mathbf u_{1:t}^{e}),\\
 \mathbf h_{1:t}^{i}&=\mathbf u_{1:t}^{i}
 +W_a^i\operatorname{GRU}_i(\mathbf u_{1:t}^{i}).
\end{align}
Both output projections $W_a^e$ and $W_a^i$ are initialized to zero, so the
motion stage begins from the frozen features rather than immediately replacing
them.

The detached state probability is embedded as
$\mathbf s_t=\phi_s(\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf q_t))$. IMU provides the
mechanical base representation, while sEMG supplies a bounded,
state-conditioned correction:
\begin{align}
 \mathbf c_t&=\tanh\!\left(\phi_c
 [\mathbf h_t^e,\mathbf s_t]\right),\\
 \gamma_t&=\sigma\!\left(\phi_\gamma
 [\mathbf h_t^i,\mathbf h_t^e,\mathbf s_t]\right),\\
 \mathbf f_t&=\operatorname{LN}
 (\mathbf h_t^i+\gamma_t\mathbf c_t).
\end{align}
The correction projection is zero-initialized and the gate bias is initialized
to $-2$. This provides a conservative IMU-centered starting point while still
allowing sEMG to modify the representation when supported by motion
supervision.

\subsubsection{Deployed and training-only heads}

Two-layer multilayer perceptrons map $\mathbf f_t$ directly to current
Cartesian position and normalized screen location. The screen prediction is
converted to physical pixels using the two display dimensions independently,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf u}_t^{\mathrm{px}}
    =(W\hat u_{x,t},H\hat u_{y,t}),
\end{equation}
with $W=1920$ and $H=1080$ in our experiments. No spatial grid or offset
parameterization is used.

For future prediction, the decoder concatenates $\mathbf f_t$ with the horizon
encoding
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf b(\tau)=[\tau,\tau^2,\sin(\pi\tau),\cos(\pi\tau)]
\end{equation}
and predicts the absolute Cartesian position at every 10-ms step through
200 ms. A separate training-only decoder receives the sEMG representation and
the same type of horizon encoding at 100-ms intervals through 1 s. It predicts
future IMU changes but is removed from the deployed output interface. In
particular, the final model does not produce a one-second Cartesian trajectory.

\subsection{Stage-II Learning Objective}
\label{subsec:stage2-loss}

Let $\rho(\cdot)$ denote element-wise smooth-$L_1$ and let
$\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal V}$ average only valid targets. Current position
is supervised in the training-standardized Cartesian space:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}=
 \left\langle\rho(\hat{\mathbf p}_t-\mathbf p_t)\right\rangle_{\mathcal V_p}.
\end{equation}
For the direct screen head, normalized residuals are scaled independently by
display width and height. Defining
$\mathbf D=\operatorname{diag}(W/100,H/100)$ and normalized trial progress
$r_t\in[0,1]$, the screen objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pixel}}=
 \frac{\sum_{t\in\mathcal V_u}a_t
 \rho\!\left(\mathbf D
 (\hat{\mathbf u}_t-\mathbf u_t)\right)}
 {\sum_{t\in\mathcal V_u}a_t},
 \qquad a_t=0.25+3.75r_t^4.
\end{equation}
The separate $x$ and $y$ scaling respects the 16:9 display geometry, while
$a_t$ emphasizes the later part of the reach where the intended screen target
should have converged.

For $\mathcal H_s=\{10,20,\ldots,200\}$ ms, dense future supervision is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{future}}=
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal H_s|}
 \sum_{h\in\mathcal H_s}
 \left\langle
 \rho(\hat{\mathbf p}_{t+h\mid t}-\mathbf p_{t+h})
 \right\rangle_{\mathcal V_{t,h}}.
\end{equation}
This objective trains trajectory prediction directly rather than deriving it
from current position or a constant-velocity assumption.

The only long-horizon auxiliary objective is sEMG-to-future-IMU prediction.
Let $\mathcal H_l=\{100,200,\ldots,1000\}$ ms, let $h^{-}$ denote the previous
horizon (zero for the first interval), and define the interval target
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\bar{\mathbf i}_{t,h}=
 \frac{1}{h-h^{-}}
 \int_{h^{-}}^{h}\mathbf i_{t+\tau}\,d\tau-\mathbf i_t.
\end{equation}
The discrete implementation averages the 100-Hz samples in each interval. The
auxiliary loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{e\rightarrow i}=
 \frac{\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_l}\lambda_h
 \left\langle\rho(
 \widehat{\Delta\bar{\mathbf i}}_{t,h}
 -\Delta\bar{\mathbf i}_{t,h})\right\rangle}
 {\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_l}\lambda_h},
 \qquad
 \lambda_h=\exp\!\left(-\frac{h}{500\ \mathrm{ms}}\right).
\end{equation}
The decoder receives no IMU representation. It therefore cannot minimize the
loss by copying current inertial features and must instead retain sEMG patterns
that predict subsequent mechanical motion. Future IMU is used only to construct
training targets.

The complete Stage-II objective is
\begin{equation}
 \boxed{
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{II}}=
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}
 +0.35\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pixel}}
 +0.50\mathcal L_{\mathrm{future}}
 +0.05\mathcal L_{e\rightarrow i}}
 \;.
\end{equation}
There is no Stage-II classification loss because the validated state classifier
is frozen. Orientation, endpoint, spatial-grid, within-cell offset,
future-arrival consistency, correction regularization, masked-sEMG
reconstruction, and one-second Cartesian intent losses are also absent.

\subsection{Optimization and Causal Inference}

Both stages use AdamW with learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay
$10^{-4}$, batch size four, and gradient-norm clipping at one. Models are
trained for at most 60 epochs with early stopping after 12 validation epochs
without improvement. Stage I selects the checkpoint with maximum validation
macro-F1. Stage II selects its checkpoint using the validation motion score.
Trials, rather than overlapping frames, are assigned to the training,
validation, and test partitions using a fixed split seed, and all normalization
statistics are estimated from the training partition.

At inference, the same causal preprocessing is applied with a two-second
history and a 300-ms warm-up. State output uses hysteresis: the gripper changes
to closed when $P(g_t=\mathrm{close})\geq0.7$, changes to open when
$P(g_t=\mathrm{close})\leq0.3$, and otherwise retains its previous state. Each
valid output contains the state probabilities, current Cartesian position,
continuous screen coordinate, and 20 future Cartesian positions through
200 ms. No VIVE sample, future wearable measurement, target label, or online
parameter update is required. The complete network contains 3,242,105
parameters, of which 2,642,136 belong to the frozen Stage-I classifier and
encoders and 599,969 are optimized in Stage II.
