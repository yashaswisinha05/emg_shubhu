\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation}

We consider causal estimation of hand state and reaching intent from four
surface electromyography (sEMG) channels and four six-axis inertial measurement
units (IMUs). At time $t$, the wearable observation is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_t=[\mathbf{e}_t,\mathbf{a}_t,\boldsymbol{\omega}_t],
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{4}$ contains the sEMG measurements and
$\mathbf{a}_t,\boldsymbol{\omega}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{12}$ contain acceleration and
angular velocity from the four IMUs. Using only observations available at or
before $t$, the model predicts the open/close state, current Cartesian
position, continuous screen location, a dense trajectory up to 200 ms, and a
lower-rate intent trajectory up to 1 s. Long-horizon motion is represented as
a displacement from the estimated current position:
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf p}^{\mathrm{intent}}_{t+h}
    =\hat{\mathbf p}_t+\Delta\hat{\mathbf p}_{t,h}.
\end{equation}

The framework contains two stages. Stage I learns a robust gripper-state
representation using multimodal causal patch encoders. Stage II freezes and
reuses these encoders and learns current and future motion through lightweight
recurrent adapters. VIVE position and future IMU measurements are used only as
supervision and are not required during inference.

\subsection{Causal Signal Processing}

Measurements are ordered by timestamp and resampled to a uniform 100-Hz grid.
Missing values are filled using the most recent past observation for at most
20 ms; longer gaps remain invalid and are accompanied by binary validity
indicators.

Each sEMG channel is processed by a fourth-order causal Butterworth band-pass
filter with lower cutoff 20 Hz and upper cutoff
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{high}}=\min(450,0.4f_s)\ \mathrm{Hz}.
\end{equation}
Trailing root-mean-square envelopes of 20 and 50 ms produce eight sEMG
features. The short window preserves transitions, while the longer window
provides a stable activation estimate. Each of the 24 IMU channels is processed
by a trailing three-sample median filter followed by a second-order causal
15-Hz low-pass filter. The DC component is retained to preserve gravity-related
orientation information.

Channel-wise normalization statistics are calculated from valid training data
only. Standardized values are clipped to $[-20,20]$ and concatenated with their
validity indicators, producing
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf e}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{16},\qquad
    \tilde{\mathbf i}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{48}.
\end{equation}
A frame contributes to the fused losses only when at least 75\% of both
modalities are valid. Screen coordinates are predicted in normalized form and
converted using
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf u}^{\mathrm{px}}_t
    =(W\hat u_{x,t},H\hat u_{y,t}),
\end{equation}
where $W=1920$ and $H=1080$.

\subsection{Stage I: Gripper-State Representation}

\subsubsection{Multiscale causal encoding}

sEMG and IMU are encoded separately because they describe complementary
processes. sEMG directly measures muscle recruitment, whereas IMU provides the
mechanical context needed to distinguish grasp activation from reaching,
rotation, and stabilization.

Each modality is projected to a 128-dimensional feature space. A local branch
uses causal depthwise convolutions with kernels of five and nine frames. A
context branch divides the signal into overlapping 16-frame patches with
stride four and processes them using a four-layer, four-head causal
transformer. Left padding and a causal attention mask prevent access to future
samples.

Fusion is performed independently for the local and context features. For
$r\in\{\mathrm{loc},\mathrm{ctx}\}$, the modality weights are
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{\alpha}^{r}_t=
 \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
 g_r\!\left(\operatorname{sg}
 [\mathbf h^{e,r}_t,\mathbf h^{i,r}_t]\right)\right),
\end{equation}
and the fused feature is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf z^r_t=\phi_r\!\left(
 [\alpha^{e,r}_t\mathbf h^{e,r}_t,
  \alpha^{i,r}_t\mathbf h^{i,r}_t,
  \mathbf h^{e,r}_t-\mathbf h^{i,r}_t,
  \mathbf h^{e,r}_t\odot\mathbf h^{i,r}_t]\right).
\end{equation}
The weighted terms retain modality-specific information, while the difference
and product describe cross-modal disagreement and agreement. Stop-gradient is
applied only to the gate input; the encoders remain trainable through the
downstream losses.

\subsubsection{State prediction}

The gripper decoder combines stable context logits with high-resolution local
logits:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\ell}^{g}_t
    =\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathrm{ctx}}_t
    +\sigma(\beta)\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathrm{loc}}_t.
\end{equation}
An additional causal sEMG-only branch reconstructs masked activation and
predicts masked state labels during Stage-I learning. This branch ensures that
the representation retains direct neuromuscular information rather than
depending entirely on IMU context. At inference, its action inputs are replaced
by learned MASK tokens.

The principal Stage-I objective is class-balanced cross entropy,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_g=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal V_g|}
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal V_g}w_{g_t}
 \log\operatorname{softmax}(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{g}_t)_{g_t},
 \qquad w_c=\frac{N}{2N_c}.
\end{equation}
Class weighting prevents the more frequent state from dominating training.
In the following, $\rho(\cdot)$ denotes the element-wise smooth-$L_1$ penalty,
and $\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal V}$ denotes an average over valid elements.
The complete Stage-I objective is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm I}={}&
 \mathcal L_g
 +0.20\mathcal L_{\mathrm{aux}}
 +0.15(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{mask}}
       +\mathcal L_{\mathrm{recon}})
 +0.03\mathcal L_{\mathrm{stable}} \nonumber\\
 &+0.20\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}^{\mathrm I}
 +0.50\mathcal L_{\mathrm{ori}}^{\mathrm I}
 +0.35\mathcal L_{\mathrm{screen}}^{\mathrm I}
 +0.20\mathcal L_{\mathrm{end}}^{\mathrm I} \nonumber\\
 &+0.10\mathcal L_{\mathrm{future}}^{\mathrm I}
 +0.05\mathcal L_{\mathrm{IMU}}^{\mathrm I}.
\end{align}
The sEMG-only auxiliary classifier is supervised by
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{aux}}
 =\left\langle
 \operatorname{CE}(\boldsymbol\ell^{e}_t,g_t)
 \right\rangle_{t\in\mathcal V_g}.
\end{equation}
This prevents all state information from being delegated to the inertial
stream. For an independently sampled set of masked action spans
$\mathcal M_g$, the masked-state loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{mask}}
 =\left\langle
 \operatorname{CE}(\boldsymbol\ell^{e,\mathrm{mask}}_t,g_t)
 \right\rangle_{t\in\mathcal M_g}.
\end{equation}
It requires the causal sEMG branch to infer a hidden state from preceding
sensor context rather than reading the corresponding action token.

For masked sEMG spans $\mathcal M_e$, the reconstruction objective is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{recon}}
 =\left\langle
 \|\hat{\mathbf e}_t-\mathbf e_t\|_2^2
 \right\rangle_{t\in\mathcal M_e}.
\end{equation}
This Stage-I-only loss regularizes the sEMG encoder against short dropouts and
encourages it to retain the temporal structure of muscle activation. It is
different from intent prediction: reconstruction estimates a hidden current
sensor representation, whereas intent predicts future hand displacement.

The stability term penalizes changes in close probability only when adjacent
labels are equal:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{stable}}
 =\left\langle
 [P_t(\mathrm{close})-P_{t-1}(\mathrm{close})]^2
 \right\rangle_{g_t=g_{t-1}}.
\end{equation}
It suppresses spurious switching without penalizing genuine transitions.

Current position and orientation are supervised in standardized Cartesian and
continuous 6-D rotation representations:
\begin{align}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}^{\mathrm I}
 &=\left\langle
 \rho(\hat{\mathbf p}_t-\mathbf p_t)
 \right\rangle_{\mathcal V_p},\\
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{ori}}^{\mathrm I}
 &=\left\langle
 \rho(\hat{\mathbf r}^{6D}_t-\mathbf r^{6D}_t)
 \right\rangle_{\mathcal V_r}.
\end{align}
These losses associate state features with the instantaneous mechanical
configuration and help separate similar muscle amplitudes produced under
different arm orientations.

Let $d(\hat{\mathbf u},\mathbf u)$ denote smooth-$L_1$ error after independently
scaling normalized horizontal and vertical coordinates to units of 100 pixels.
The Stage-I screen loss combines its fused prediction with auxiliary direct and
endpoint-derived predictions:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{screen}}^{\mathrm I}
 =\left\langle a_t^{\mathrm I}
 [d(\hat{\mathbf u}_t,\mathbf u)
 +0.2d(\hat{\mathbf u}^{d}_t,\mathbf u)
 +0.2d(\hat{\mathbf u}^{e}_t,\mathbf u)]\right\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $a_t^{\mathrm I}=0.25+2.75r_t^2$. This associates the representation with
the interaction target and gives greater importance to predictions made after
more of the reach has been observed.

The Stage-I endpoint objective is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{end}}^{\mathrm I}={}&
 2.5\left\langle(1+r_t^2)
 \rho\!\left(
 \frac{(\hat{\mathbf p}^{e}_t-\mathbf p^{e})
       \odot\boldsymbol\sigma_p}{0.1\ \mathrm m}
 \right)\right\rangle \\
 &+\left\langle
 \rho(\hat{\mathbf r}^{e,6D}_t-\mathbf r^{e,6D})
 \right\rangle.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The physical scaling gives equal meaning to the three Cartesian axes, while
the increased late-trial weight emphasizes the final interaction location.

For $\mathcal H_s=\{10,20,\ldots,200\}$ ms, the Stage-I future-pose loss is
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{future}}^{\mathrm I}={}&
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal H_s|}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_s}
 \left\langle\rho(
 \hat{\mathbf p}_{t+h\mid t}-\mathbf p_{t+h})\right\rangle\\
 &+\frac{1}{|\mathcal H_s|}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_s}
 \left\langle\rho(
 \hat{\mathbf r}^{6D}_{t+h\mid t}-\mathbf r^{6D}_{t+h})
 \right\rangle.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
It prevents the source representation from explaining only the present frame.
Finally, the Stage-I future-IMU loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{IMU}}^{\mathrm I}
 =\frac{1}{|\mathcal H_s|}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_s}
 \left\langle\rho\!\left(
 \widehat{\Delta\mathbf i}_{t,h}
 -(\mathbf i_{t+h}-\mathbf i_t)
 \right)\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
This auxiliary target links the learned state to subsequent mechanical change.
The Stage-I kinematic heads regularize representation learning; the final
Stage-II system does not deploy its orientation, endpoint, or Stage-I future
outputs. After checkpoint selection, the complete classifier and both modality
encoders are frozen.

\subsection{Stage II: State-Conditioned Motion Model}

\subsubsection{Shared features and residual adaptation}

Because the two stages may use different population statistics, Stage-II
inputs are mapped into the frozen classifier's normalization space:
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf z_c=\mathbf z_m\odot
 \frac{\boldsymbol\sigma_m}{\boldsymbol\sigma_c}
 +\frac{\boldsymbol\mu_m-\boldsymbol\mu_c}
       {\boldsymbol\sigma_c}.
\end{equation}
The frozen classifier is evaluated once, and its modality-specific context
features are reused by Stage II.

Let $\mathbf u^e_t,\mathbf u^i_t\in\mathbb R^{128}$ denote these frozen
features. Separate two-layer causal GRUs learn residual temporal adaptations:
\begin{align}
 \mathbf h^e_{1:t}&=\mathbf u^e_{1:t}
 +W^e_a\operatorname{GRU}_e(\mathbf u^e_{1:t}),\\
 \mathbf h^i_{1:t}&=\mathbf u^i_{1:t}
 +W^i_a\operatorname{GRU}_i(\mathbf u^i_{1:t}).
\end{align}
The output projections are initialized to zero, so training begins from the
frozen Stage-I representation.

The frozen open/close probability is embedded as
$\mathbf s_t=\phi_s(\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf q_t))$. IMU acts as the
mechanical base and sEMG supplies a bounded state-dependent correction:
\begin{align}
 \mathbf c_t&=\tanh\!\left(
 \phi_c([\mathbf h^e_t,\mathbf s_t])\right),\\
 \gamma_t&=\sigma\!\left(
 \phi_\gamma([\mathbf h^i_t,\mathbf h^e_t,\mathbf s_t])\right),\\
 \mathbf f_t&=\operatorname{LN}
 (\mathbf h^i_t+\gamma_t\mathbf c_t).
\end{align}
The correction layer is zero-initialized and the gate initially favors the IMU
base. Consequently, sEMG alters the motion representation only when it reduces
the supervised error.

\subsubsection{Prediction heads}

Current Cartesian position is predicted from $\mathbf f_t$ using a two-layer
MLP. A separate screen head predicts the continuous normalized coordinate
directly from the fused representation:
\begin{equation}
 \hat{\mathbf u}_t=\phi_{\mathrm{screen}}(\mathbf f_t),
\end{equation}
which is converted independently along the horizontal and vertical axes when
reported in pixels.

A shared future decoder combines $\mathbf f_t$ with the horizon encoding
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf b(\tau)=[\tau,\tau^2,\sin(\pi\tau),\cos(\pi\tau)]
\end{equation}
and predicts absolute Cartesian positions every 10 ms through 200 ms. The
long-horizon decoder additionally receives the explicit sEMG state
$\mathbf h^e_t$ and predicts displacement every 100 ms through 1 s:
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\hat{\mathbf p}_{t,h}
 =\phi_{\mathrm{intent}}
 ([\mathbf f_t,\mathbf h^e_t,\mathbf b(h)]).
\end{equation}
The explicit sEMG pathway allows early muscle activation to contribute to
intent prediction before its mechanical consequence is fully observed by the
IMU.

\subsection{Final Motion Objective}
\label{subsec:final-loss}

The final Stage-II objective is written parametrically as
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{II}}
 =\sum_{k\in\mathcal T}\eta_k\mathcal L_k,
 \label{eq:stage-two-objective}
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal T=\{\mathrm{pos},\mathrm{screen},\mathrm{future},
              \mathrm{cons},\mathrm{intent}\}.
 \end{aligned}
\end{equation}
These terms respectively supervise current Cartesian position, screen
location, the dense 200-ms trajectory, temporal consistency, one-second
motion intent. The corresponding coefficients $\eta_k$ were 1.00, 0.35, 0.50,
0.10, and 0.05, in the order listed above.

$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}$ is a smooth-$L_1$ loss on current Cartesian
position:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}
 =\left\langle\rho(
 \hat{\mathbf p}_t-\mathbf p_t)\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
Smooth-$L_1$ reduces sensitivity to occasional VIVE and synchronization
outliers. The screen loss is evaluated in physical pixel space:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{screen}}
 =\left\langle
 a_t\rho\!\left(
 \frac{(\hat{\mathbf u}_t-\mathbf u_t)\odot(W,H)}{100}
 \right)\right\rangle,
 \qquad a_t=0.25+3.75r_t^4,
\end{equation}
where $r_t$ is normalized trial progress. Independent axis scaling respects
the display aspect ratio, while $a_t$ emphasizes convergence near interaction
without removing early supervision.

The short-horizon loss directly supervises all 20 future positions between 10
and 200 ms:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{future}}
 =\frac{1}{|\mathcal H_s|}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_s}
 \left\langle\rho(
 \hat{\mathbf p}_{t+h\mid t}-\mathbf p_{t+h})\right\rangle,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal H_s=\{10,20,\ldots,200\}$ ms. The consistency loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{cons}}
 =\frac{1}{|\mathcal H_s|}\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_s}
 \left\langle\rho\!\left(
 \hat{\mathbf p}_{t+h\mid t}
 -\operatorname{sg}(\hat{\mathbf p}_{t+h\mid t+h})
 \right)\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
The first term supplies direct future supervision; the second makes successive
predictions agree when the forecast time arrives. Stop-gradient prevents the
consistency path from moving the later current-position estimate.

The long-horizon intent loss supervises displacement at
$h\in\{100,200,\ldots,1000\}$ ms:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{intent}}=
 \frac{\sum_h\lambda_h
 \left\langle\rho\!\left(
 \Delta\hat{\mathbf p}_{t,h}
 -(\mathbf p_{t+h}-\mathbf p_t)\right)\right\rangle}
 {\sum_h\lambda_h},
 \qquad \lambda_h=\exp[-h/(500\ \mathrm{ms})].
\end{equation}
Displacement targets reduce dependence on participant-specific workspace
variation. The decay preserves supervision at 1 s while assigning greater weight
to the more predictable near horizons.

No Stage-II state loss is used because the authoritative gripper classifier is
frozen. Orientation and final 3-D endpoint prediction are also disabled in the
final model. Stage II uses AdamW with learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, weight
decay $10^{-4}$, batch size four, gradient clipping at one, and early stopping
after 12 stale validation epochs. The complete network contains 3,654,603
parameters, of which 3,001,102 are frozen and 653,501 are optimized in Stage II.

\subsection{Causal Inference}

The deployment pipeline applies the same causal preprocessing and retains two
seconds of history with a 300-ms warm-up. Open/close output is stabilized using
hysteresis: the state changes to closed when $P(\mathrm{close})\geq0.7$, changes
to open when $P(\mathrm{close})\leq0.3$, and is otherwise retained. The model
returns current position, screen location, 20 future positions through 200 ms,
and ten intent positions through 1 s. No VIVE measurement, future wearable
sample, masked target, or online update is used during inference.
