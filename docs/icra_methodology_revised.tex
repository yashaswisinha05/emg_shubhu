\section{Methodology}
\label{sec:methodology}

\subsection{Problem Formulation and Model Overview}

We consider causal estimation of hand state and reaching intent from four
surface electromyography (sEMG) channels and four six-axis inertial measurement
units (IMUs). At acquisition time $t$, the raw wearable observation is
\begin{equation}
    \mathbf{x}_t=
    [\mathbf{e}_t,\mathbf{a}_t,\boldsymbol{\omega}_t],
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{4}$ contains one sEMG measurement from each
sensor and $\mathbf{a}_t,\boldsymbol{\omega}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{12}$ contain the
three-axis acceleration and angular velocity of the four IMUs. The model uses
only measurements available at or before $t$ and produces: (i) the binary
gripper state $\hat g_t\in\{\mathrm{open},\mathrm{close}\}$, (ii) current
Cartesian hand position $\hat{\mathbf p}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{3}$, (iii) continuous
screen location $\hat{\mathbf u}_t\in[0,1]^2$, (iv) a dense Cartesian trajectory
at 10-ms intervals up to 200 ms, and (v) a lower-rate intent trajectory at
100-ms intervals up to 1 s. The long-horizon output is represented as a
displacement from the estimated current position,
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf p}^{\mathrm{intent}}_{t+h}
    =\hat{\mathbf p}_t+\Delta\hat{\mathbf p}_{t,h}.
\end{equation}

The model is learned in two stages. Stage I learns a robust multimodal
open/close representation using causal patch-transformer encoders. Stage II
freezes and reuses those encoders, introduces lightweight recurrent adapters,
and learns current and future motion. Reusing the same encoders avoids a second
raw-signal pathway, while freezing them prevents motion-regression gradients
from degrading the validated gripper-state representation. VIVE measurements
and future IMU observations are used only to construct supervision targets;
neither is required during deployment.

\subsection{Causal Signal Representation}
\label{subsec:causal-preprocessing}

Samples are ordered by acquisition timestamp and mapped to a uniform 100-Hz
grid. Invalid and duplicate timestamps are removed. A missing sensor value is
replaced by its most recent past observation only when that observation is no
more than 20 ms old. Longer gaps remain invalid and are represented by a zero
value together with a binary validity indicator. This bounded past filling
prevents missing measurements from being interpreted as genuine zero-valued
sensor activity.

Each sEMG channel is processed by a fourth-order causal Butterworth band-pass
filter with lower cutoff 20 Hz and upper cutoff
\begin{equation}
    f_{\mathrm{high}}=\min(450,0.4f_s)\ \mathrm{Hz},
\end{equation}
where $f_s$ is the acquisition frequency. Trailing root-mean-square envelopes
of 20 and 50 ms are subsequently computed. The shorter envelope preserves
rapid state transitions, whereas the longer envelope provides a less noisy
estimate of sustained muscle recruitment. The four physical channels
therefore produce eight causal sEMG features.

Each of the 24 inertial channels is processed by a trailing three-sample median
filter followed by a second-order causal 15-Hz Butterworth low-pass filter. The
median stage suppresses isolated spikes, while the low-pass stage removes
high-frequency measurement noise. The DC component is retained because gravity
provides useful forearm-orientation context. No centered window or zero-phase
filter is used in either modality.

Channel-wise means and standard deviations are estimated from valid training
samples only. Standardized values are clipped to $[-20,20]$ and concatenated
with their validity indicators, yielding
\begin{equation}
    \tilde{\mathbf e}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{16},\qquad
    \tilde{\mathbf i}_t\in\mathbb{R}^{48}.
\end{equation}
A frame contributes to a fused task objective only when at least 75\% of both
modalities are valid. VIVE Cartesian position is standardized independently
along each axis using training-set statistics. Screen coordinates remain
normalized during prediction and are converted to pixels using
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\mathbf u}^{\mathrm{px}}_t
    =(W\hat u_{x,t},H\hat u_{y,t}),
\end{equation}
with $W=1920$ and $H=1080$. Independent axis scaling is essential because the
display is not square.

\subsection{Stage I: Multimodal Gripper-State Representation}
\label{subsec:stage-one}

\subsubsection{Causal multiscale encoders}

Separate sEMG and IMU encoders are used because the two modalities describe
different physical processes. sEMG measures muscle recruitment and provides
direct evidence of opening and closing, whereas the IMUs describe forearm
motion, rotation, and stabilization. The latter context helps distinguish an
actual hand-state change from muscle activity caused by reaching or postural
stabilization.

Each packed input is projected into a $d=128$ feature space. A local branch
uses causal depthwise convolutions with kernels of five and nine frames to
retain sharp temporal changes. In parallel, overlapping patches of 16 frames
with stride four are processed by a four-layer, four-head transformer. Left
padding makes every patch end at its reported time, and a strictly causal
attention mask prevents access to future patches. The local and patch features
are combined to obtain modality-specific context features
$\mathbf h^{e,r}_t$ and $\mathbf h^{i,r}_t$ at resolutions
$r\in\{\mathrm{ctx},\mathrm{loc}\}$.

At each resolution, a learned gate computes
\begin{equation}
 \boldsymbol{\alpha}^{r}_t=
 \operatorname{softmax}\!\left(
 g_r\!\left(\operatorname{sg}
 [\mathbf h^{e,r}_t,\mathbf h^{i,r}_t]\right)\right),
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}$ denotes stop-gradient. Detaching only the gate input
prevents the encoders from reshaping their features merely to manipulate the
gate; gradients from downstream task losses still update both encoders. The
fused feature is
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf z^r_t=\phi_r\!\left(
 \left[
 \alpha^{e,r}_t\mathbf h^{e,r}_t,
 \alpha^{i,r}_t\mathbf h^{i,r}_t,
 \mathbf h^{e,r}_t-\mathbf h^{i,r}_t,
 \mathbf h^{e,r}_t\odot\mathbf h^{i,r}_t
 \right]\right).
\end{equation}
The first two terms retain modality-specific evidence, the difference exposes
disagreement, and the Hadamard product represents agreement between muscle
activation and observed motion.

\subsubsection{State decoder and sEMG alignment}

The main gripper decoder combines stable contextual logits with
frame-resolution local logits:
\begin{equation}
    \boldsymbol{\ell}^{g}_t
    =\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathrm{ctx}}_t
    +\sigma(\beta)\boldsymbol{\ell}^{\mathrm{loc}}_t.
\end{equation}
The contextual branch stabilizes sustained open and closed states, whereas the
local causal convolution sharpens state transitions. The blend parameter is
initialized at $\beta=-2$, so training begins with the less noisy contextual
estimate.

An auxiliary sEMG-only alignment branch is included to ensure that the state
representation retains neuromuscular evidence rather than delegating the
decision entirely to IMU context. At each frame, an sEMG token and an
open/close action token are interleaved and processed by a width-64 causal
transformer. During inference every action token is replaced by a learned MASK
token; consequently, the branch cannot read the true state. A zero-initialized
projection adds the auxiliary correction to the main logits. This makes the
branch an initial no-op and allows it to influence the classifier only after it
learns predictive sEMG structure.

\subsubsection{Stage-I objective and rationale}

Let $\rho(\cdot)$ be the element-wise smooth-$L_1$ penalty and
$\langle\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal V}$ denote an average over valid elements. The
primary gripper loss is class-balanced cross entropy,
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_g=-\frac{1}{|\mathcal V_g|}
 \sum_{t\in\mathcal V_g}w_{g_t}
 \log\operatorname{softmax}(\boldsymbol{\ell}^{g}_t)_{g_t},
 \qquad w_c=\frac{N}{2N_c}.
\end{equation}
This term directly optimizes open/close discrimination, while inverse-frequency
weights prevent the more frequent state from dominating the decision.

The auxiliary state loss $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{aux-state}}$ applies the same
classification target to the sEMG-only branch. Its purpose is not to replace
multimodal classification, but to preserve state information in the muscle
representation even when the IMU provides an easier shortcut.

For randomly selected temporal spans, action tokens are replaced by MASK and
sEMG features are withheld. The masked objectives are
\begin{align}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{masked-state}}
 &=\operatorname{CE}(\hat g_t,g_t),\quad t\in\mathcal M_g,\\
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{EMG-recon}}
 &=\left\langle
 \|\hat{\mathbf e}_t-\mathbf e_t\|_2^2
 \right\rangle_{t\in\mathcal M_e}.
\end{align}
Masked-state prediction encourages temporal context rather than frame-wise
memorization. Masked sEMG reconstruction regularizes the Stage-I encoder and
makes it less sensitive to short signal dropouts. This reconstruction is used
only while learning the source classifier; it is not part of Stage II.

The probability-stability loss is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{stable}}=
 \left\langle
 [P_{t}(\mathrm{close})-P_{t-1}(\mathrm{close})]^2
 \right\rangle_{g_t=g_{t-1}}.
\end{equation}
It penalizes rapid probability changes only when adjacent labels agree,
reducing false open/close switching without suppressing genuine transitions.

The classifier is initially learned inside a larger multitask network. Current
position, orientation, screen target, final interaction position, short-horizon
pose, and future IMU-change losses expose the shared encoders to both the hand
state and the mechanical circumstances in which that state occurs. The source
objective is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{I}}={}&
 \mathcal L_g
 +0.20\mathcal L_{\mathrm{aux-state}}
 +0.15(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{masked-state}}
       +\mathcal L_{\mathrm{EMG-recon}})
 +0.03\mathcal L_{\mathrm{stable}} \nonumber\\
 &+0.20\mathcal L_{\mathrm{current-pos}}
 +0.50\mathcal L_{\mathrm{orientation}}
 +0.35\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pixel}}
 +0.20\mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}} \nonumber\\
 &+0.10\mathcal L_{\mathrm{future-pose}}
 +0.05\mathcal L_{\mathrm{future-IMU}}.
\end{align}
The motion-related terms are auxiliary at this stage: they prevent the state
encoder from learning a participant- or session-specific contraction template
that lacks kinematic context. In particular,
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{current-pos}}$ associates the representation with the
instantaneous hand location, while $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{orientation}}$
distinguishes forearm rotations that may produce similar sEMG amplitudes.
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pixel}}$ associates the features with the intended screen
target. The endpoint term is
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{endpoint}}
 =2.5\mathcal L_{\mathrm{final-pos}}
 +\mathcal L_{\mathrm{final-orientation}},
\end{equation}
so its position component receives additional emphasis while retaining final
orientation as geometric context. $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{future-pose}}$
supervises position and orientation every 10 ms through 200 ms and therefore
discourages a representation that explains only the present frame.
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{future-IMU}}$ asks the future latent to predict inertial
change over the same interval, linking the learned state to subsequent
mechanical motion. These endpoint, orientation, and future-motion heads provide
representation supervision to Stage I; they are not deployed as outputs of the
final Stage-II model. After validation-based checkpoint selection, the complete
Stage-I classifier and both modality encoders are frozen.

\subsection{Stage II: State-Conditioned Residual Motion Model}
\label{subsec:stage-two}

\subsubsection{Normalization bridge and shared encoding}

The Stage-I classifier and Stage-II motion model may use statistics computed
from different source populations. Let
$\mathbf z_m=(\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\mu_m)/\boldsymbol\sigma_m$ be a
motion-normalized feature. Before it enters the frozen classifier, it is mapped
analytically into the classifier's normalization space:
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf z_c=\mathbf z_m\odot
 \frac{\boldsymbol\sigma_m}{\boldsymbol\sigma_c}
 +\frac{\boldsymbol\mu_m-\boldsymbol\mu_c}
       {\boldsymbol\sigma_c}.
\end{equation}
This bridge prevents normalization mismatch from being mistaken for a change
in muscle state. Validity flags are passed unchanged. The frozen network is
kept in evaluation mode and evaluated once per input sequence; Stage II reuses
its modality-specific context features rather than encoding the raw signals a
second time.

\subsubsection{Residual GRU adaptation and conditional correction}

Let $\mathbf u^e_t,\mathbf u^i_t\in\mathbb R^{128}$ denote the frozen sEMG and
IMU context features. Separate two-layer unidirectional GRUs learn temporal
residuals,
\begin{align}
 \mathbf h^e_{1:t}&=\mathbf u^e_{1:t}
 +W^e_a\operatorname{GRU}_e(\mathbf u^e_{1:t}),\\
 \mathbf h^i_{1:t}&=\mathbf u^i_{1:t}
 +W^i_a\operatorname{GRU}_i(\mathbf u^i_{1:t}).
\end{align}
Both GRUs have width 128 and dropout 0.1 between their recurrent layers. The
output projections $W^e_a$ and $W^i_a$ are initialized to zero. Thus, Stage II
starts from the validated frozen representation and learns only task-specific
temporal corrections.

The frozen classifier supplies
$\mathbf q_t=\operatorname{softmax}(\boldsymbol\ell^g_t)$. Its detached state
embedding is $\mathbf s_t=\phi_s(\operatorname{sg}(\mathbf q_t))$. The IMU
feature acts as the mechanical base, while the sEMG feature produces a bounded,
state-aware correction:
\begin{align}
 \mathbf c_t&=\tanh\!\left(
 \phi_c([\mathbf h^e_t,\mathbf s_t])\right),\\
 \gamma_t&=\sigma\!\left(
 \phi_\gamma([\mathbf h^i_t,\mathbf h^e_t,\mathbf s_t])\right),\\
 \mathbf f_t&=\operatorname{LN}
 (\mathbf h^i_t+\gamma_t\mathbf c_t).
\end{align}
The final correction layer is zero-initialized. The gate is initialized with
zero weights and bias $-2$, initially producing a small correction. This
asymmetry reflects the observed role of the modalities: IMU is reliable for
the current mechanical state, while sEMG is allowed to modify that state where
neuromuscular evidence improves the supervised objective.

\subsubsection{Task-specific decoders}

Current position is predicted from $\mathbf f_t$ by a two-layer multilayer
perceptron. The screen decoder predicts a distribution $\pi_{t,k}$ over an
internal $3\times3$ grid and a bounded two-dimensional offset
$\boldsymbol\delta_{t,k}$ for every grid cell:
\begin{equation}
 \hat{\mathbf u}_t=\sum_{k=1}^{9}\pi_{t,k}
 \operatorname{clip}_{[0,1]}
 (\mathbf m_k+\boldsymbol\delta_{t,k}),
 \qquad
 \boldsymbol\pi_t=\operatorname{softmax}
 (\boldsymbol\ell^{\mathrm{grid}}_t).
\end{equation}
This internal $3\times3$ basis is a differentiable parameterization of a
continuous coordinate; it is independent of the external $3\times2$ grid used
to report six-cell pointing accuracy.

For dense future motion, a shared decoder combines $\mathbf f_t$ with
\begin{equation}
 \mathbf b(\tau)=[\tau,\tau^2,\sin(\pi\tau),\cos(\pi\tau)],
 \qquad \tau=h/(200\ \mathrm{ms}),
\end{equation}
and directly predicts the standardized absolute position at
$h\in\{10,20,\ldots,200\}$ ms. A second decoder predicts longer-horizon
displacements at $h\in\{100,200,\ldots,1000\}$ ms:
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\hat{\mathbf p}_{t,h}
 =\phi_{\mathrm{intent}}
 ([\mathbf f_t,\mathbf h^e_t,\mathbf b(h/1000\ \mathrm{ms})]).
\end{equation}
The explicit $\mathbf h^e_t$ input prevents long-horizon intent from accessing
muscle information only through the deliberately small residual correction.

\subsection{Stage-II Motion and Intent Objectives}
\label{subsec:stage-two-losses}

The Stage-II objective is
\begin{align}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{II}}={}&
 1.00\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}
 +0.35\mathcal L_{\mathrm{pixel}}
 +0.15\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grid}}
 +0.10\mathcal L_{\mathrm{offset}} \nonumber\\
 &+0.50\mathcal L_{\mathrm{future200}}
 +0.10\mathcal L_{\mathrm{consistency}}
 +0.05\mathcal L_{\mathrm{intent-pos}} \nonumber\\
 &+0.05\mathcal L_{\mathrm{EMG}\rightarrow\mathrm{future\text{-}IMU}}
 +0.01\mathcal L_{\mathrm{correction}}.
\end{align}
The frozen Stage-I logits are the authoritative gripper estimate; therefore no
Stage-II classification loss is applied. Orientation and final 3-D endpoint
heads are also disabled in the final model.

\paragraph{Current-position loss.}
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pos}}=
 \left\langle\rho(
 \hat{\mathbf p}_t-\mathbf p_t)\right\rangle_{\mathcal V_p}.
\end{equation}
This is the principal instantaneous kinematic objective. Smooth-$L_1$ is used
instead of squared error to reduce the influence of occasional VIVE outliers or
imperfect synchronization while retaining a differentiable metric loss.

\paragraph{Pixel, grid, and offset losses.}
The continuous screen error is evaluated in units of 100 pixels:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{pixel}}=
 \frac{\sum_t a_t v_t\,
 \rho\!\left(
 \frac{(\hat{\mathbf u}_t-\mathbf u_t)\odot(W,H)}{100}
 \right)}{\sum_t a_t v_t},
 \qquad a_t=0.25+3.75r_t^4,
\end{equation}
where $v_t$ indicates a valid target and $r_t\in[0,1]$ is normalized progress
through the recorded trial. Physical pixel scaling gives the horizontal and
vertical axes their correct 1920:1080 cost. The late-progress weighting asks
the estimate to converge near interaction time while retaining nonzero early
supervision. Nine-class cross entropy
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{grid}}$ encourages the correct coarse region, and
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{offset}}$ applies smooth-$L_1$ to the offset of the
ground-truth cell. Their combination reduces averaging between spatially
separated targets while preserving continuous pixel output.

\paragraph{Dense 200-ms future-position loss.}
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{future200}}=
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal H_{200}|}
 \sum_{h\in\mathcal H_{200}}
 \left\langle\rho(
 \hat{\mathbf p}_{t+h\mid t}-\mathbf p_{t+h})
 \right\rangle,
 \quad
 \mathcal H_{200}=\{10,20,\ldots,200\}\ \mathrm{ms}.
\end{equation}
Direct supervision at every short horizon teaches motion evolution rather than
only a distant endpoint. This term is assigned the largest auxiliary weight
because its removal caused the largest degradation in the corresponding
future-position ablation.

\paragraph{Prediction-to-arrival consistency.}
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{consistency}}=
 \frac{1}{|\mathcal H_{200}|}\sum_h
 \left\langle\rho\!\left(
 \hat{\mathbf p}_{t+h\mid t}
 -\operatorname{sg}(\hat{\mathbf p}_{t+h\mid t+h})
 \right)\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
This term requires a prediction made at time $t$ to agree with the model's
detached current-position estimate when the predicted time arrives. It reduces
temporal inconsistency without allowing the future decoder to move the later
current-position target through the consistency path.

\paragraph{One-second intent-position loss.}
For $\mathcal H_{1000}=\{100,200,\ldots,1000\}$ ms, the target is displacement
rather than absolute position:
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{intent-pos}}=
 \frac{\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_{1000}}\lambda_h
 \left\langle\rho\!\left(
 \Delta\hat{\mathbf p}_{t,h}
 -(\mathbf p_{t+h}-\mathbf p_t)\right)\right\rangle}
 {\sum_{h\in\mathcal H_{1000}}\lambda_h},
 \qquad \lambda_h=\exp[-h/(500\ \mathrm{ms})].
\end{equation}
Displacement supervision isolates intended motion from participant-specific
workspace offsets. Exponential weighting acknowledges increasing uncertainty
at longer horizons without removing the 1-s training signal.

\paragraph{sEMG-to-future-IMU loss.}
To encourage prospective neuromuscular structure, an auxiliary decoder receives
only $\mathbf h^e_t$ and predicts a future inertial summary. For consecutive
long-horizon boundaries $h_{k-1}$ and $h_k$, the target is
\begin{equation}
 \Delta\bar{\mathbf i}_{t,k}=
 \frac{1}{h_k-h_{k-1}}
 \sum_{j=h_{k-1}+1}^{h_k}\mathbf i_{t+j}-\mathbf i_t,
\end{equation}
with indices expressed on the 100-Hz grid. The corresponding smooth-$L_1$ loss
$\mathcal L_{\mathrm{EMG}\rightarrow\mathrm{future\text{-}IMU}}$ asks whether
muscle activation predicts subsequent mechanical change. The current IMU
feature is deliberately excluded from this auxiliary head to prevent a copying
shortcut. Interval averaging emphasizes low-frequency motion and avoids forcing
the network to reproduce noisy raw future samples. This training-only decoder
is not required during deployment.

\paragraph{Correction regularization.}
\begin{equation}
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{correction}}=
 \left\langle\|\mathbf c_t\|_2^2\right\rangle.
\end{equation}
The penalty discourages unnecessary sEMG corrections and preserves the strong
IMU mechanical baseline when muscle activity supplies no additional evidence.
Its small coefficient reflects its role as a stability prior rather than a
primary prediction objective.

Stage II uses AdamW with learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$, weight decay
$10^{-4}$, batch size four, gradient-norm clipping at one, and early stopping
after 12 validation epochs without improvement. The complete inference network
contains 3,654,603 parameters: 3,001,102 frozen Stage-I parameters and 653,501
trainable Stage-II parameters.

\subsection{Causal Inference}

During deployment, the same causal filters, 100-Hz resampling, normalization,
clipping, and validity tests are applied incrementally. The implementation
retains two seconds of context and requires 300 ms of warm-up. The frozen
classifier produces open and close probabilities. A hysteretic state machine
changes to closed when $P(\mathrm{close})\geq0.7$, changes to open when
$P(\mathrm{close})\leq0.3$, and otherwise retains its previous state. The
system returns the current Cartesian position, continuous screen coordinate,
20 future positions through 200 ms, and ten intent positions through 1 s. No
VIVE measurement, future wearable observation, masked target, or online
parameter update is used during inference.
